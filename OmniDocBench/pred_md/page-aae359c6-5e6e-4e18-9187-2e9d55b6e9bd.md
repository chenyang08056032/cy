2.9. Fully faithful centralizing functors. In order to use the technology developed above, we must have some way of constructing objects of  \( \mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{K}^{b}(\mathcal{M})) \) . Many examples (see §3) can be provided with the following relatively elementary result.

Theorem 2.21. Let A be a k-linear monoidal category and M an  \( (A, \mathcal{A}) \star \) -bimodule category, and let  \( Z \in K^{b}(\mathcal{M}) \)  be given. Assume that  \( L_{Z}, R_{Z}: A \to K^{b}(\mathcal{M}) \)  are fully faithful and have the same essential image. Then there is a monoidal autoequivalence  \( \Phi: A \to A \)  such that  \( Z \star X \simeq \Phi(X) \star Z \) , naturally in X  \( \in A \) . In other words, Z has the structure of an object in the Drinfeld centralizer  \( \mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A}, \Phi^{K^{b}}(\mathcal{M})) \) , where  \( \Phi^{K^{b}}(\mathcal{M}) \)  denotes  \( K^{b}(\mathcal{M}) \)  with left action twisted by  \( \Phi \) .
