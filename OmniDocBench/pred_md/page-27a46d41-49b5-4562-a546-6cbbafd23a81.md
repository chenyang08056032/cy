
Remark 2.2. We have used the version of the vector representation  \( V_{q_{1}}^{*} \)  that is dual to the representation conventionally used in the literature (see e.g. [4, 5]). In our convention this means that the operator  \( R(x|v) \)  acts on a wavefunction that corresponds to a diagram sitting above the crossing in the picture.

Using Theorem 2.1 it is easy to find the R-matrix acting on  \( (\mathcal{V}_{q_{1}}^{*})^{\otimes N} \otimes \mathcal{F}_{q_{2},q_{3}}^{(1,0)}(v) \) : it is given by the composition of N R-matrices (2.1) along the horizontal line. The result is a difference operator in  \( x_{i} \)   \( i = 1, \ldots, N \)  which is also a vertex operator acting on  \( \mathcal{F}_{q_{2},q_{3}}^{(1,0)}(v) \) .

Definition 2.3. Let  \( \mathcal{O}(\vec{x}|v):(\mathcal{V}_{q_{1}}^{*})^{\otimes N}\to(\mathcal{V}_{q_{1}}^{*})^{\otimes N} \)  be matrix element of the R-matrix acting on  \( (\mathcal{V}_{q_{1}}^{*})^{\otimes N}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{q_{2},q_{3}}^{(1,0)}(v) \)  between the vacuum states in the Fock representation:

 \[\begin{align*}\mathcal{O}_{N}(\vec{x}|v)&=\langle\mathcal{O},v|\vec{\mathcal{R}}|_{(\mathcal{V}_{q_{1}}^{*})^{\otimes N}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{q_{2},q_{3}}^{(1,0)}(v)}|\mathcal{O},v\rangle=\\&=\langle\mathcal{O},v|\prod_{i=1}^{\rightarrow N}R_{q_{2},q_{3}}^{q_{1}}(x_{i}|v)|\mathcal{O},v\rangle=\quad\langle\mathcal{O},v|\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xrightarrow{\quad}\xleftarrow{\quad}\end{align*}\] 

where  \( x_{i} \)  is understood as the variable on which the functions in the i-th  \( V_{q_{1}}^{*} \)  tensor factor depend. We call  \( \mathcal{O}(\vec{x}|v) \)  the transfer matrix.

Definition 2.4. Trigonometric Ruijsenaars-Schneider Hamiltonians \( ^{1} \)  are the following difference operators

 \[H_{k}^{\mathrm{t R S},(N)}(\vec{x},q_{1},q_{2})=q_{2}^{-\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}\sum_{\substack{I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}\\ |I|=k}}\prod_{i\in I}\prod_{j\not\in I}\frac{q_{2}^{-1}x_{i}-x_{j}}{x_{i}-x_{j}}\prod_{i\in I}q_{1}^{x_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}},\qquad k=0,\ldots,N.\] 

Trigonometric RS Hamiltonians commute [3] and their common polynomial eigenfunctions are given by Macdonald symmetric polynomials  \( P_{\lambda}(\vec{x}) \) :

 \[H_{k}^{\mathrm{t R S},(N)}(\vec{x},q_{1},q_{2})P_{\lambda}^{(q_{1},q_{2})}(\vec{x})=e_{k}(\vec{w})P_{\lambda}^{(q_{1},q_{2})}(\vec{x}),\] 

where  \( u_{i}=q_{1}^{\lambda_{i}}q_{2}^{i-N} \)  for  \( i=1,\ldots,N \) ,  \( e_{k} \)  are elementary symmetric functions and  \( \lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{N}) \)  is a partition (Young diagram). In what follows we will mostly be interested in the eigenfunctions for generic parameters  \( u_{i} \) ; these are non-polynomial.

We notice that  \( \mathcal{O}_{N}(\vec{x}|v) \)  is the generating function of tRS Hamiltonians:

Theorem 2.5.

 \[(F_{q_{1},q_{2}}(\vec{x}))^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{N}(\vec{x}|v)F_{q_{1},q_{2}}(\vec{x})=q_{2}^{\frac{N(N-1)}{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{N}(-v/q_{1})^{k-N}H_{k}^{\mathrm{t R S},(N)}(\vec{x},q_{1},q_{2})q_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}},\] 

where

 \[F_{q_{1},q_{2}}(\vec{x})=\prod_{k<l}^{N}\left[x_{k}^{-\frac{1}{2n}q_{2}}\frac{\left(\frac{x_{l}}{x_{k}};q_{1}\right)}{\left(q_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{x_{l}}{x_{k}};q_{1}\right)_{\infty}}\right].\]
