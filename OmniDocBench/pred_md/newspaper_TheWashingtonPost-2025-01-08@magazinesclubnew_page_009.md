
# 7.1-magnitude quake kills at least 95 in southern Tibet


Emergency workers search for survivors Tuesday in the aftermath of a quake in a remote area of southern Tibet.

JIGME DORJE/AGENCE FRANCE-PRESSE/GETTY IMAGES

BY LVIV LAU
AND CHRISTINE RIOS-TOFF

An earthquake killed at least 95 people and left 130 injured after it struck a remote area of southern Tibet near the border with Nepal at dawn on Tuesday, Chinese state media reported.

Liu Huazhong, deputy mayor of Shigatse, broke into tears during a news conference Tuesday afternoon while announcing the human toll and the damage.

Emergency responders were still assessing the extent of the damage in Qinghai province from the quake, which struck at 7:10 a.m. Beijing time, state broadcaster CCTV said. The U.S. Geological Survey put the quake at 7.1 in magnitude and said it was felt in France when seismic data indicated a quake of that magnitude.
The tremor brought down more than 1,700 homes in a county that is home to ethnic Tibetan people, according to the Chinese government-installed Tibet’s Ministry of Culture. The broader Himalayas, the epicenter of the quake, were also felt as far as Tibet and the holy seat of the second-most-important religious leader in Tibetan Buddhism, the Dalai Lama, the People’s Republic of China said.

The China Earthquake Networks Center has recorded multiple aftershocks and expects more in coming days. That set of seismic activity followed a major quake and a power outage in another Tibetan town, the Huangtu and Eerding districts, on Friday.

Chinese leader Xi Jinping called for an all-out rescue and relief response effort to minimize casualties. More than 9,000 people were hospitalized as a result of the quake, the country’s director of public information, reported.
