In what follows, we will write  \( Hom_{G} \)  instead of  \( Hom_{ZG} \)  and  \( \otimes_{G} \)  instead of  \( \otimes_{ZG} \) . Because of the special role of the trivial G-module Z, the augmentation

 \[\varepsilon:\mathbb{Z}G\to\mathbb{Z},\] 

defined by

 \[\varepsilon:\sum_{x\in G}m_{x}x\mapsto\sum_{x\in G}m_{x},\] 

is important. Recall that we have seen, in Exercise 8.37 on page 573, that  \( \varepsilon \)  is a surjective ring homomorphism, and so its kernel, G, is a two-sided ideal in ZG, called the augmentation ideal. Thus, there is an exact sequence

 \[0\to\mathcal{G}\to\mathbb{Z}G\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}\mathbb{Z}\to0.\] 

Proposition 10.103. Let G be a group with augmentation ideal G. As an abelian group, G is free abelian with basis  \( G - 1 = \{x - 1 : x \in G, x \neq 1\} \) .

Proof. An element  \( u = \sum_{x} m_{x} x \in ZG \)  lies in  \( \ker \varepsilon = G \)  if and only if  \( \sum_{x} m_{x} = 0 \) . Therefore, if  \( u \in G \) , then

 \[u=u-\left(\sum_{x}m_{x}\right)1=\sum_{x}m_{x}(x-1).\] 

Thus, G is generated by the nonzero x - 1 for  \( x \in G \) .

Suppose that  \( \sum_{x\neq1}n_{x}(x-1)=0 \) . Then  \( \sum_{x\neq1}n_{x}x-(\sum_{x\neq1}n_{x})1=0 \)  in ZG, which, as an abelian group, is free abelian with basis the elements of G. Hence,  \( n_{x}=0 \)  for all  \( x\neq1 \) . Therefore, the nonzero x-1 comprise a basis of G.

We begin by examining homology groups.

Proposition 10.104. If A is a G-module, then

 \[H_{0}(G,A)=\mathbb{Z}\otimes_{G}A\cong A/\mathcal{G}A.\] 

Proof. By definition,  \( H_{0}(G, A) = \operatorname{Tor}_{0}^{\mathbb{Z}G}(\mathbb{Z}, A) = \mathbb{Z} \otimes_{G} A \) . Applying the right exact functor  \( \otimes_{G} A \)  to the exact sequence

 \[0\to\mathcal{G}\to\mathbb{Z}G\to\mathbb{Z}\to0\] 

gives exactness of the first row of the following commutative diagram:

 \[\begin{array}{c}\mathcal{G}\otimes_{G}A\xrightarrow{\quad}\mathbb{Z}G\otimes_{G}A\xrightarrow{\quad}\mathbb{Z}\otimes_{G}A\xrightarrow{\quad}0\\\downarrow\quad\downarrow\quad\downarrow\\\mathcal{G}A\xrightarrow{\quad}A\xrightarrow{\quad}A/\mathcal{G}A\xrightarrow{\quad}0\end{array}\] 

The two solid vertical arrows are given by  \( u \otimes a \mapsto ua \) . By Proposition 8.93, there is an isomorphism  \( Z \otimes_{G} A \cong A / G A \) .
