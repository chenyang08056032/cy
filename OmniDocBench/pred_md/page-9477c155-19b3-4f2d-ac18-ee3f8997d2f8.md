
## G.1.3. BOUNDS ON THE SECOND MOMENT ALONG THE FINITE-PARTICLE SYSTEMS

In this section, we show that along the finite-particle dynamics (16) and algorithm (20), the distributions remain in  \( \mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{2dN}) \) .

Under Assumption 1, recall from Theorem 35 (in Section H) that there exists a unique equilibrium point  \( z^{*} = (x^{*}, y^{*}) \in \mathbb{R}^{2d} \)  which satisfies  \( \nabla V(z^{*}) = 0 \) . Define  \( \mathbf{z}^{*} = (x^{*}, \ldots, x^{*}, y^{*}, \ldots, y^{*}) \in \mathbb{R}^{2dN} \) . Then by construction,  \( b^{\mathbf{Z}}(\mathbf{z}^{*}) = 0 \) , where  \( b^{\mathbf{Z}} \)  is the vector field defined in (17).

Lemma 31 Assume Assumption 1. Suppose  \( Z_{t} \sim \rho_{t}^{Z} \)  evolves following the finite-particle dynamics (16) in  \( R^{2dN} \)  from  \( Z_{0} \sim \rho_{0}^{Z} \in \mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{2dN}) \) . Then  \( \rho_{t}^{Z} \in \mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{2dN}) \)  for all  \( t \geq 0 \) .

Proof We note that  \( \rho_{t}^{Z} \)  is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure on  \( R^{2dN} \)  by virtue of the Brownian motion component in the dynamics (16). We now show that the second moment of  \( \rho_{t}^{Z} \)  remains finite for all  \( t \geq 0 \) . Since  \( Z_{t} \)  evolves following the dynamics (16), its density  \( \rho_{t}^{Z} \)  evolves following the Fokker-Planck equation:

 \[\frac{\partial\rho_{t}^{\mathbf{Z}}}{\partial t}=-\nabla\cdot\left(\rho_{t}^{\mathbf{Z}}b^{\mathbf{Z}}\right)+\tau\Delta\rho_{t}^{\mathbf{Z}}.\] 

From this, we can compute, using integration by parts:

 \[\begin{align*}\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{t}^{Z}}\left[\left\|\mathbf{Z}_{t}-\mathbf{z}^{*}\right\|^{2}\right]&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2dN}}\frac{\partial\rho_{t}^{\mathbf{Z}}(\mathbf{z})}{\partial t}\left\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{z}^{*}\right\|^{2}d\mathbf{z}\\&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2dN}}\left(-\nabla\cdot\left(\rho_{t}^{\mathbf{Z}}b^{\mathbf{Z}}\right)(\mathbf{z})+\tau\Delta\rho_{t}^{\mathbf{Z}}(\mathbf{z})\right)\left\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{z}^{*}\right\|^{2}d\mathbf{z}\\&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2dN}}\rho_{t}^{\mathbf{Z}}(\mathbf{z})\left\langle b^{\mathbf{Z}}(\mathbf{z}),\nabla\left(\left\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{z}^{*}\right\|^{2}\right)\right\rangle d\mathbf{z}+\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2dN}}\rho_{t}^{\mathbf{Z}}(\mathbf{z})\Delta\left(\left\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{z}^{*}\right\|^{2}\right)d\mathbf{z}\\&=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2dN}}\rho_{t}^{\mathbf{Z}}(\mathbf{z})\left\langle b^{\mathbf{Z}}(\mathbf{z}),\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{z}^{*}\right\rangle d\mathbf{z}+\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2dN}}\rho_{t}^{\mathbf{Z}}(\mathbf{z})\left(4dN\right)d\mathbf{z}\\&=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2dN}}\rho_{t}^{\mathbf{Z}}(\mathbf{z})\left\langle b^{\mathbf{Z}}(\mathbf{z})-b^{\mathbf{Z}}(\mathbf{z}^{*}),\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{z}^{*}\right\rangle d\mathbf{z}+4\tau dN\\&\leq-2\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2dN}}\rho_{t}^{\mathbf{Z}}(\mathbf{z})\left\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{z}^{*}\right\|^{2}d\mathbf{z}+4\tau dN\\&=-2\alpha\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{t}^{Z}}\left[\left\|\mathbf{Z}_{t}-\mathbf{z}^{*}\right\|^{2}\right]+4\tau dN\end{align*}\] 

where the inequality above uses the property that  \( -b^{Z} \)  is  \( \alpha \) -strongly monotone, from Lemma 12. We can write the differential inequality above equivalently as:

 \[\frac{d}{d t}\left(e^{2\alpha t}\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{t}^{Z}}\left[\left\|\mathbf{Z}_{t}-\mathbf{z}^{*}\right\|^{2}\right]\right)\leq e^{2\alpha t}4\tau d N.\] 

Integrating from 0 to t and rearranging the result gives:

 \[\begin{align*}\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{t}^{Z}}\left[\left\|\mathbf{Z}_{t}-\mathbf{z}^{*}\right\|^{2}\right]&\leq e^{-2\alpha t}\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{0}^{Z}}\left[\left\|\mathbf{Z}_{0}-\mathbf{z}^{*}\right\|^{2}\right]+\frac{\left(1-e^{-2\alpha t}\right)}{\alpha}2\tau d N\\&\leq\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{0}^{Z}}\left[\left\|\mathbf{Z}_{0}-\mathbf{z}^{*}\right\|^{2}\right]+\frac{2\tau d N}{\alpha}.\end{align*}\]
