Therefore h is a Jacobi-Koszul-Vinberg structure on  \( (A,\phi_{0}) \)  if and only if  \( \tilde{h} \)  is a Koszul-Vinberg structure on  \( (\tilde{A},\tilde{\phi}_{A}^{0},\tilde{\rho}_{A}^{\phi_{0}}) \) .

DEFINITION 2.8.  \( \tilde{h} \)  in Theorem 2.7 is called the Koszul-Vinbergization of a Jacobi-Koszul-Vinberg structure h on  \( (A,\phi_{0}) \) .

Now we define a Jacobi-Koszul-Vinberg manifold as a symmetric analogue of a Jacobi manifold.

DEFINITION 2.9. A Jacobi-Koszul-Vinberg structure on an affine manifold  \( (M, \nabla) \)  is a pair  \( (h, E) \)  in  \( \Gamma(S^{2}TM) \times \mathfrak{X}(M) \)  satisfying, for any  \( \alpha, \beta, \gamma \)  in  \( \Omega^{1}(M) \) ,

 \[\left(\mathrm{i}\right)\left(\nabla_{h^{\sharp}\alpha}h\right)\left(\beta,\gamma\right)-\left\langle\alpha,E\right\rangle h\left(\beta,\gamma\right)=\left(\nabla_{h^{\sharp}\beta}h\right)\left(\alpha,\gamma\right)-\left\langle\beta,E\right\rangle h\left(\alpha,\gamma\right),\] 

 \[\left(\mathrm{i i}\right)\left(\nabla_{E}h\right)\left(\beta,\gamma\right)-\left\langle\gamma,\nabla_{h^{\sharp}\beta}E\right\rangle+\left\langle\beta,E\right\rangle\left\langle\gamma,E\right\rangle=0,\] 

 \[\left(\mathrm{i i i}\right)\nabla_{E}E=0.\] 

 \( (M, \nabla, h, E) \)  is called a Jacobi-Koszul-Vinberg manifold.

Furthermore, because of Example 1.13 and Proposition 2.2,  \( ((TM\oplus\mathbb{R},\nabla,\operatorname{pr}_{1}),(0,1)) \)  is a Jacobi-left-symmetric algebroid over M.
