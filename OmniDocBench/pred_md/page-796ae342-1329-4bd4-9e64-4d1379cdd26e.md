The first and the third named authors have proved in [HW25] that, under mild assumptions on the location of poles, the  \( \omega_{n}^{(0)} \)  satisfy linear and quadratic loop equations and obey blobbed topological recursion [BS17]. Loop equations in the quartic analogue of the Kontsevich model lead so such  \( \omega_{n}^{(0)} \)  for the special involution  \( \iota z = -z \) . See [HW25, BHW22].

Although surprising at first sight,  \( \omega_{2}^{(0)} \)  is symmetric: one has  \( B(w,\iota z)=B(\iota w,z) \)  and  \( B(w,z)=B(\iota w,\iota z) \) . This paper addresses the important question whether all  \( \omega_{n}^{(0)}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}) \)  defined by (1) and (2) are symmetric in their arguments  \( z_{i} \) . This symmetry is by no means obvious; it has not been proved so far. We will show that symmetry is reduced to the following purely combinatorial statement about integer partitions into a given number of parts:

Theorem 1. Let  \( \mathbf{P}_{k}(n) \)  be the set of partitions of an integer  \( n \geq 1 \)  into  \( 1 \leq k \leq n \)  parts. For any given integers  \( (s, k, l) \)  with  \( k, l \geq 0 \)  and  \( s \geq \max(k + l, 1) \)  and any given partition  \( \nu \in \mathbf{P}_{s}(2s - k - l) \)  one has

 \[\begin{align*}s!=\sum_{\substack{(r-l)+p_{1}+\ldots+p_{l}=s\\ r-l\geq k,\ p_{1},\ldots,p_{l}\geq1}}\sum_{\mu\in\mathbf{P}_{r-l}(2r-k-2l)}\sum_{\rho_{1}\in\mathbf{P}_{p_{1}}(2p_{1}-1)}\cdots\sum_{\rho_{l}\in\mathbf{P}_{p_{l}}(2p_{l}-1)}\\ \times\binom{\nu}{\mu,\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{l}}(r-l)!(p_{1}-1)!\cdots(p_{l}-1)!.}\end{align*}\] 

By  \( \binom{\nu}{\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{l}} \)  we denote the multinomial coefficient of partitions,

 \[\begin{align*}\binom{\nu}{\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{l}}&=\lim_{N\to\infty}\prod_{k=1}^{N}\binom{h_{k}}{g_{k,1},\ldots,g_{k,l}}\\if\quad\nu&=(\ldots,\underbrace{2,\ldots,2}_{h_{2}},\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{h_{1}}),\qquad\mu_{i}=(\ldots,\underbrace{2,\ldots,2}_{g_{2,i}},\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{g_{1,i}}),\end{align*}\] 

where  \( \binom{h_{k}}{g_{k,1},\ldots,g_{k,l}} \)  are the usual multinomial coefficients which vanish unless  \( h_{k}=g_{k,1}+\ldots+g_{k,l} \) .

We give two examples at the end of sec. 5 and a proof of this theorem in sec. 6. This theorem is a main ingredient of the proof of the symmetry:

Theorem 2. All differentials  \( \omega_{n}^{(0)}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}) \)  defined by (1) and (2) are symmetric in their arguments  \( z_{i} \) .

Theorem 2 is proved by reduction to Theorem 1 in sec. 5.
